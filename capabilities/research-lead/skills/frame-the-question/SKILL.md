# Frame the question

A report answers a question someone has to act on. Frame it before you plan: an angle
planned for the wrong question is a researcher wasted.

## 1. Read first
- the request verbatim, and who asked;
- your soul's knowledge and the organisation's earlier reports: is it answered already, or
  in part? (the "already known?" check of `/plan-the-angles` starts here);
- what the request leaves open.

## 2. The frame
Four lines:
~~~
Decision:        <what the person who asked will decide or do with the answer>
Would change it: <the findings that would move that decision one way or the other>
Answered:        <what the report must show for them to accept it, how sure, and how current>
Out of scope:    <what the work deliberately leaves out>
~~~
"Answered" is checkable: named options compared on named criteria, a figure with its
range, a yes or a no with its conditions. "Everything about X" is not a frame.

## 3. Agree it with the person who asked
Send them the frame once, in the effort's thread, with your reading of each line the
request left open. It is one plain question ("Is this what you need answered?"), not a
card: the decision and what would change it are facts only they have, and the frame is
theirs to correct. Set needs-input (`oats instance attention --message "<one line>"`) and
end your turn. Meanwhile run the "already known?" check; plan no angle the answer could
change. Where the request already states all four lines, record the frame, tell them in
one line the frame you are working to, and go on.

## 4. Record it
The frame goes at the top of the effort's record, with their answer (`DECISION by
<person>, <link>: <their words>`), and clear needs-input. It stays the test of the work:
`/write-the-report` writes against it, and the person accepts the report against it. A
change to the frame later is theirs; record it the same way.

## When a report doesn't yet answer it
Read their reason against the frame. The frame was wrong: frame it again with them, as
above. The report fell short of it: plan the angles it lacks (`/plan-the-angles`).
