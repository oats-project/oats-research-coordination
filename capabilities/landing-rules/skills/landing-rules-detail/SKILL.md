# The landing rules, in detail

Your briefing's landing rules name who accepts a report, what lands it, the one-way doors
kept for people and where things are kept. This skill holds the definitions behind them.
Where the two differ, a change you recorded since your spawn wins, then the briefing.

## A verdict, and CHECKED
- **A verdict counts** only with its Checked line: the attacks the verifier ran, and what
  each opened. A claim with no verdict, or a verdict with no Checked line, has not been
  checked: it goes to a fresh verifier.
- **`CHECKED at <revision>`** is the lead's note on the effort's record, with the verdicts,
  one line each. It asserts that every claim the report leans on has its verdict at that
  revision, and that each was acted on as the landing rules say. The lead never checks a
  claim itself, an angle it read itself included: the note records the verifier's checks.

Why: a claim is checked by someone other than its author, and a check that doesn't say
what it checked is no check.

## VERIFIED, for a helper's piece and a specialist's area
Whoever specified a piece of the effort reads it once, against its brief's "done", before
the effort leans on it: a helper's scoped piece at its exact reference, a specialist's
area findings at their checked revision. The note is `VERIFIED at <reference>: <what was
checked against each "done" item>`. It verifies the piece, never its claims: the claims the
report takes from it still go to a verifier. Why: whoever specified the work holds its
design, and nobody verifies their own work.

## What carries to a new revision
- **The verdicts:** a revision that changes a claim the report leans on, or adds one, gets
  a fresh verifier for that claim, never the one that checked the old wording, and a new
  `CHECKED`. A revision that changes no claim (wording, layout, a fixed link) carries them:
  the lead reads the delta and writes `CHECKED at <new revision>: delta since <old
  revision> read, no claim changed`.
- **A claim from a specialist's checked findings,** taken into the report unchanged (its
  wording, its source, its qualifiers), keeps its verdict. One the lead narrows, combines
  or rewords is a changed claim, and a conclusion the lead draws across areas, or from
  several carried claims together, is a new claim: each goes to a fresh verifier.
- **An acceptance and a yes to publish** are of one revision. A revision that changes or
  adds a claim after an acceptance goes back to the person who accepts the report; a yes
  to publish never carries to another revision.
- **A `VERIFIED` note or a green light** carries to no other revision: whoever gave it
  reads the delta and gives it again at the new one. A verifier's verdict never stands in
  for either.
- **Direction people already gave** (the frame, a card's answer) holds through every
  revision: nobody is asked to decide it again.

## A maintainer's green light, or another reader
Only where your landing rules ask for one, for the report's area.
- **The call:** by message, from the lead, with the report at its checked revision, the
  frame, the `CHECKED` note, and the verdicts it acted on.
- **The verdict:** by message to the lead. `GREEN LIGHT at <revision>` with what it
  checked; `RETURN at <revision>` with findings; or `ESCALATE`, when a direction question
  must go to whoever decides it first. Another reader answers as its rule says.
- **What a maintainer checks:** the area's direction, scope against the frame, coherence
  with what the area already holds, and the evidence the answer rests on. Never the
  claims again: the verifier checked those.
- **Two areas:** each area's green light.

Why, where an organisation asks for it: one seat holds an area's direction across
parallel efforts.

## The model floor
The floor in your briefing binds every child, an OATS child or a native subagent, and it
is checked before model diversity.
- It binds the model a child would actually launch on, wherever that comes from: a soul's
  default or a machine's override that is below the floor is not used. Spawn that child
  on a model at or above the floor instead, your own by default, and tell your person
  once that the configuration is below the floor, naming the soul and the machine.
- A model whose tier you can't establish is below the floor.
- A child that checks work runs on another family's model only where one at or above the
  floor is available; otherwise on your own model, and you say that diversity was lost.
