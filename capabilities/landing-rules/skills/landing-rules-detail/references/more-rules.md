# The landing rules: who does the work, before the angles, records

## Who does the work
- The lead frames the question, plans the angles, decides who researches each, writes the
  report and lands it. It researches a single small angle itself. A researcher reads an
  angle where one protects the lead's context, reads in parallel or specialises, one angle
  each, and a question with several angles always goes to researchers.
- The verifier checks the claims the report leans on: always a fresh instance, briefed to
  disprove. Every report gets one, a small question's included.
- All are the lead's children, and every task one gets is closed: a researcher, a verifier
  or a probe reports once and retires itself; a helper with a scoped piece once its piece
  is verified; a specialist when its area is done.
- The lead never checks its own claims, an angle it researched itself included, and no
  researcher or specialist checks its own.

Why: one agent holds the whole question, an extra agent costs a context and a brief, and a
claim is checked by someone other than its author.

## Before the angles
Where an area asks for its maintainer's green light, a direction question (the frame, an
angle the area has decided against) goes to that maintainer before the angles are sent, so
its green light holds no surprise.

## Records
Where your briefing says they are kept:
- **An effort record:** one item per effort, its status at the top, in words a person can
  read cold: what it is, where it stands, who is on it (instance and seat or role), what
  waits on whom. It also holds what your tasks layer's items never do: the frame, the
  angles and who read each, the probes and what each found, the specialists and their
  areas, the interfaces agreed between them, each report revision with its verdicts, and
  the people's decisions.
- **A finding outside your work:** one record, sent to its area's owner.

The tasks layer holds outcomes only: an effort, a finding to act on.

## After a change to these rules
Where the workspace has oats.framework's `oats.coordination-setup`, its set-landing-rules
skill writes a change, on the yes of the person who owns the area.
Whoever wrote the change messages each standing instance (the dispatchers) and the lead of
each open effort, with the change at its commit. Each lead tells its running children of a
change that bears on them: its specialists and helpers, and its researchers and verifiers
where it bears on their briefs (the sources they may use, say). Each recipient records the
change, at its commit, in its state file (a lead on its effort's record too), and from then
on reads these rules with the changes it recorded, compaction or not.
